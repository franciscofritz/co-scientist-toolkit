# Review Rubrics

Use this file's review priorities as a fallback. Whenever the venue's current reviewer guidelines or review form can be found, use those (see SKILL.md Step 2). The form specifies the dimension names, scoring scales and recommendation labels, which change from year to year and are not recorded here. Where the form and this file disagree, use the form.

If the user names a venue not listed here, look up its reviewer guidelines. If none can be found, adapt the closest entry below and say which one you used.

---

## NeurIPS (Conference on Neural Information Processing Systems)

Dimensions usually assessed: quality or soundness, clarity or presentation, significance, originality or contribution.

Review priorities:
- Novelty and significance of the contribution
- Technical correctness
- Clarity and quality of presentation
- Experimental methodology, where applicable: error bars, multiple runs and reproducibility
- Theory papers: at least proof sketches in the main body, with full proofs available
- Broader impact and ethics considerations

---

## ICML (International Conference on Machine Learning)

Dimensions usually assessed: soundness, significance, novelty, clarity.

Review priorities:
- Methodological and algorithmic novelty
- Strong experimental validation
- Theoretical grounding
- Practical relevance
- Reproducibility

---

## ICLR (International Conference on Learning Representations)

Dimensions usually assessed: soundness, presentation, contribution.

Review priorities:
- Understanding and representation of data
- An explanation of why methods work, as well as evidence that they do
- Clear experimental protocols
- Theoretical insight, even for empirical papers
- Specific criticism that authors can answer, as reviews and author replies are public

---

## COLT (Conference on Learning Theory)

Dimensions usually assessed: technical correctness, significance, novelty, clarity.

Review priorities:
- Correctness above all else, with full proofs expected in the main body or a supplement
- Tightness of bounds and comparison with known lower bounds
- Novel proof techniques, not only new applications of existing ones
- Connections to other areas of theoretical computer science and mathematics
- Clear, elegant results that an expert can follow
- No experiments are needed

---

## AAAI (Association for the Advancement of Artificial Intelligence)

Dimensions usually assessed: significance, soundness, novelty, clarity, relevance to the conference.

Review priorities:
- Broad relevance to AI, not only machine learning
- Clear practical motivation
- Solid experimental evaluation
- Accessibility to a broader AI audience
- Ethics considerations

---

## Nature / Science / PNAS

Dimensions: qualitative assessment, without scores.

Review priorities:
- Significance: first assess whether the work changes how people think about the field.
- Novelty
- Quality of evidence for the conclusions
- Broad impact beyond the immediate subfield
- Clarity for scientists outside the subfield
- Rigorous experimental design and statistical rigour
- Reproducibility
- Figures that present much of the argument

---

## JMLR (Journal of Machine Learning Research)

Dimensions: qualitative assessment.

Review priorities:
- Technical quality: correctness and depth
- Completeness and thoroughness of experiments and proofs
- Comparison with the state of the art
- Long-term impact over trendy topics
- Clear, readable exposition without unnecessary text
- A complete, polished body of work, more thorough than a conference paper

---

## Generic / Unspecified Venue

When no venue is specified, assess soundness, significance, novelty, clarity and reproducibility, and give an overall assessment. Choose a simple scoring scale and a recommendation scale. State both in the review and say that they are generic.

Apply common academic standards: correct claims, fair comparisons, honest limitations, clear writing.
